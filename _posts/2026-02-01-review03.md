## What is this paper regarding?

This paper demonstrate a overall process of 'Learning agile locomotion for quadruped robot' focusing on reducing sim-to-real gap which is discrepancies between physical simulator and real world and implement agile locomotion gait, such as trotting and galloping, controlling the two major factors which impact to sim-to-real gap below. 
1. high simulator fidelity: system identification, accurate actuator modeling, simulating latency. 
2. Robust Controller: randominizing the physical environment, designing a compact observation space, adding perturbations


Especially in Reinforcement Learning, narrowing this gap is significantly cruicial, since control policies which are learned in a physical simulator are deployed to hardware. In this process, low fidelity(huge sim-to-real gap) could lead failures in deployment from simulator to hardware.

## Critical Thinking & Takeaway
#### **Overcoming the Reality Gap**

Reality Gap is the major obstacles to applying deep RL in robotics. 

In system identification, inaccurate actuator models and lack of latency modeling are two major causes of the reality gap. 

Robust Controller can be improved by injecting noise, perturbing the simulated robot, leveraging multiple simulators using domain radomization and dynamic randomization.

#### **Robot Platform And Physics Simulation**

robot platform is the Minitaur from Ghost Robotics (Figure 1 bottom), a quadruped robot with eight direct-drive actuator

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/study-paper-review03/fig2.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" %}

Nvidia Jetson TX2 is high-level controller which performs neural network policies learned from deep RL. Also STM32 ARM microcontroller is low-level controller which excute position control of motor. The TX2 is interfaced with the microcontroller through UART communication. At every control step, the sensor measurements are collected at the microcontroller and sent back to the TX2, where they are fed into a neural network policy to decide the actions to take. These actions are then transmitted to the microcontroller and executed by the actuators (Figure 2).

#### **Learning Locomotive Controller**


This paper formulate locomotion control as a Partially Observable Markov Decision Process (POMDP) and solve it using a policy gradient method. 

An MDP is a tuple $(S, A, r, D, P_{sas'}, \gamma)$

where:

$S$ is the state space; $A$ is the action space

$r$ is the reward function

$D$ is the distribution of initial states $s_0$

$P_{sas'}$ is the transition probability

$\gamma \in [0, 1]$ is the discount factor

The problem is partially observable because certain states such as the position of the Minitaur's base and the foot contact forces are not accessible due to lack of corresponding sensors. The robot cannot sense all state of the robot. Thus At every control step, a partial observation $o \in O$, rather than a complete state $s \in S$, is observed. Reinforcement learning optimizes a policy $\pi : O \mapsto A$ that maximizes the expected return (accumulated rewards) $R$.

$$
\pi^* = \arg \max_\pi E_{s_0 \sim D}[R_\pi(s_0)]
$$


#### **Narrowing The Reality Gap**

As I mentioned before, sim-to-real gap can impact failure of deployment RL model to hardware. To reduce this gap, improving simulation fidelity and learning robust controller are the solutions.
